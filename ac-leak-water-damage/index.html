AC Leak Water Damage in Oshkosh: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Pricing

How the Quote Is Built

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.

How We Fix It

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
