Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Oshkosh Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Oshkosh and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Our crew returns daily, using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to track moisture until the structure is completely dry and mold growth is prevented. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
  • Consider flood insurance even if you are not in a designated flood zone
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Signs It Is Time for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water pooled on any floor of the building, no matter where it came from
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
